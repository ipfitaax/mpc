Who Is MPC For?

Your Next Step After High School

MPC is designed for students and young adults who want to build a practical professional skill in information technology. You may be a recent high-school graduate, a learner changing career direction, or someone who wants structured IT training before entering the workplace.

You do not need to know everything before you begin. The program takes students from essential computer knowledge to practical IT tasks in a clear sequence. You receive guidance, opportunities to practise, and support in preparing for your first professional opportunity.

About MPC

Practical IT Training for Your Professional Future

Mogadishu Professional Certificate provides practical training in information technology and professional skills. Our goal is to help learners understand essential IT concepts, practise useful technical tasks, complete meaningful projects, and prepare for the next stage of their education or career.

MPC connects classroom learning with real-world application. Students should not only memorize definitions; they learn how to approach a technical problem, use appropriate tools, explain their work, and continue learning after the program ends.

MPC waxay isku xirtaa waxbarashada fasalka iyo adeegsiga nolosha dhabta ah. Ardaygu ma xifdiyo qeexid keliya — wuxuu bartaa sida loo xalliyo dhibaato farsamo, loo isticmaalo qalabka saxda ah, loona sharaxo shaqadiisa.

Two Clear Pathways

Choose the Program That Matches Your Goal

MPC offers two study pathways for learners beginning an IT career. Both include practical learning and career preparation. The one-year program provides broader development and more time for practice. The six-month program provides a focused, intensive route for students who want to build essential skills in a shorter period.

One-Year Program Curriculum

What You Will Learn

A structured pathway of nine modules. Each module ends with practical evidence you keep in your portfolio.

ModuleMain topicsStudent evidence
1. Digital and Computer FoundationsOperating systems, files, productivity tools, typing, internet use, digital safety, and basic troubleshooting.A digital productivity task and a basic computer troubleshooting checklist.
2. Computer Hardware and SupportComputer components, installation, maintenance, software setup, user support, and troubleshooting methods.A hardware identification exercise and support-ticket simulation.
3. Networking FoundationsNetwork types, IP addressing, devices, cabling, switching, routing concepts, Wi-Fi, and troubleshooting.A small network diagram and a basic network configuration exercise.
4. CCNA-Oriented NetworkingCore concepts aligned with the CCNA 200-301 learning direction, subject to the official curriculum and exam plan.Network design, configuration practice, and troubleshooting documentation.
5. Data and Digital ProductivityData collection, cleaning, spreadsheets, basic analysis, charts, reporting, and data presentation.A cleaned dataset, analysis workbook, and short report.
6. Cybersecurity FoundationsPassword safety, access control, common threats, safe browsing, device security, and responsible technology use.A basic security checklist and risk-awareness presentation.
7. Professional CommunicationWritten communication, teamwork, customer service, punctuality, problem explanation, and workplace conduct.A professional email, support conversation, and teamwork assessment.
8. Career and Employment PreparationCV writing, portfolio building, interview practice, job-search methods, professional networking, and application follow-up.A completed CV, portfolio, mock interview, and personal development plan.
9. Final Applied ProjectA practical project combining technical learning and professional presentation.A final project, written documentation, and presentation.
Optional specialization subjects. Where instructors and equipment are available, the one-year program may include introductory software development, web design, database fundamentals, cloud concepts, or cybersecurity practice.

How We Teach

Learn by Doing It

IT is a practical profession. At MPC students learn concepts and then use them. Lessons include demonstrations, exercises, troubleshooting tasks, small projects, group activities, and opportunities to explain technical work clearly.

You finish the program with more than a certificate. You have evidence of what you can do: project files, reports, diagrams, presentations, and a portfolio that supports future applications.

Waxbarashadu waa ficil. Ardayga MPC wuxuu bartaa fikradda, ka dibna wuu adeegsadaa — jimicsi, xallin dhibaato, mashruucyo yaryar, iyo sharraxaad casri ah.

Our Learning Model

Stage 1

Learn

The instructor explains the concept and demonstrates the correct process.

Stage 2

Practise

Students complete a guided task individually or in a group.

Stage 3

Apply

Students solve a realistic problem or create a useful project.

Stage 4

Explain

Students document and present their work professionally.

Stage 5

Improve

The instructor gives feedback and the student strengthens the result.

Career Preparation

We Help You Prepare for the Workplace

Completing training is an important step, but beginning a career also requires confidence, communication, evidence of ability, and a plan. MPC supports students as they prepare to present themselves professionally and search for suitable opportunities.

Our career support helps students prepare a CV, organize a practical portfolio, practise interviews, improve professional communication, identify suitable entry-level roles, and understand how to submit and follow up on applications.

StageSupport provided by MPCYour responsibility

2. PrepareSupport CV writing, portfolio organization, communication, and interview practice.Complete the work and respond to feedback.
3. DemonstrateUse projects and practical tasks to show evidence of ability.Maintain and explain the portfolio honestly.
4. ApplyExplain suitable entry-level roles, applications, and professional follow-up.Apply consistently and provide accurate information.
5. ContinueEncourage further learning, certification, mentorship, and skill development.Continue building skills after graduation.

Possible Entry-Level Directions

Depending on your skills and the local opportunity environment, possible directions include IT support assistant, computer technician trainee, junior network support, help-desk assistant, data-entry or reporting assistant, office technology assistant, laboratory assistant, or internship trainee.

These are possible directions, not guaranteed outcomes.
