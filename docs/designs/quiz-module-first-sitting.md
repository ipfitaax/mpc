## Problem Statement

The quiz module is complete and entirely uncommitted: 3,410 lines across nine new
files (`lib/quiz.php` 824, `tests/QuizTest.php` 721, `admin/quiz-edit.php` 649,
`quiz.php` 366, `database/migrations/004-assessment.sql` 240, `admin/quizzes.php`
202, `lib/student-page.php` 144, `admin/quiz-grades.php` 142, `quizzes.php` 122),
plus ~1,095 lines of changes across 17 tracked files. Zero students have sat a
paper. Zero instructors have authored one.

The question is not whether the code works — it has 721 lines of tests and has
been mutation-checked. The question is whether the first instructor who opens
`admin/quiz-edit.php` ever opens it a second time.

## Demand Evidence

Strength: **still none verified**, unchanged since 2026-08-29. Asked directly

What *is* now established, and was not before:

- **Assessment already happens.** Instructors set and mark papers on paper;
  results are told to students verbally or written on a sheet. This is the first
  time the status quo has been established rather than assumed, and it is a

- **Scale: 15–25 students, one class.** Marking is roughly forty minutes.
- **Intakes are rolling — a given course recurs every month or two.**

## Status Quo

Paper. The instructor writes a paper, prints or hands it out, marks it by hand,
and communicates results verbally or on a sheet that does not survive the term.

Paper's genuine advantages, which this module has to beat rather than dismiss:
it needs no login, no device, no connectivity and no training, and it has never
once refused a student at the door.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**The user is one instructor**, teaching one class of 15–25, on a course that
comes round again every month or two. Not "students" — if no paper is authored,
`quizzes.php` renders an empty list and the entire module is a login with
nothing behind it.

**The wedge is transcription speed.** The decisive finding of this session came
from a single word in the answer "instructors *set* and mark papers on paper":
the questions already exist. Written, sequenced, with distractors that have been
tried on real students. The instructor is not authoring, they are
**transcribing** — and transcription cost is a property of the input UI, not of
the feature.

Today `admin/quiz-edit.php` takes one question per POST round-trip
(`action=add_question`, lines 218–282). Twenty questions is twenty form
submissions. That is the worst possible shape for bulk entry, and it is the only
thing standing between a working module and a returning user.

The payoff then arrives twice:

- **Marking pays the same evening.** It is what the instructor named as the
  pain, and same-day payoff is what gets a tool adopted.
- **Reuse pays within the term.** `quizzes.course_id` (`004-assessment.sql:102`,
  indexed `ix_quiz_course (course_id, is_published)`) binds a paper to the
  *course*, not the intake — so one transcribed paper is automatically live for
  every future intake of that course. With rolling intakes the second sitting is
  weeks away, not next year.

Marking is the hook; reuse is the barb. Earlier in this session they were framed
as competing wedges, which was wrong.

## Constraints

- **PHP 8.4, no framework, no build step, server-rendered.** The office tool is
  deliberately not the dc/omelette runtime the marketing site uses, because that
  runtime loads React from unpkg and fails on a bad connection.
- **The database does not deploy.** `.cpanel.yml` copies files only. Migrations
  are run by hand over SSH from `~/repositories/mpc` with `MPC_CONFIG` set.
- **The GRANT section of `004-assessment.sql` is part of the migration.** The
  application connects as a restricted user; a `GRANT` does not reach forward to
  tables created later. Skip it and the screens load, list papers correctly, and
  refuse every edit — which reads as a code bug.
- **Deploy is manual, two clicks** in cPanel. A `git push` alone changes nothing.
- **An attempt is spent when the paper is opened, not submitted.**
- **Grades are not editable**, by design — no `DELETE` on `quiz_attempts`, no
  `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on `quiz_answers`.
- **Marking is all-or-nothing on multi-select**, and a question with no correct
  option is excluded from the total.

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent with no view of the conversation was given the
problem, the session's answers and the four premises, and asked to break one. It
was dispatched specifically because this project's learnings record
`mpc-explanation-column-breaks-best-attempt-scoring` (confidence 9/10), a
permanent append-only scoring flaw that two prior reviews missed and only a cold
read caught.

**Its steelman:** not a quiz engine but *an institutional memory for
assessment*. Because papers bind to the course, each one becomes permanent
institutional property that survives the instructor leaving; the gradebook's real
value is the *empty* column — who hasn't sat — which no paper process produces.

**Its key insight, and the most valuable single observation of the session:**

> "Instructors **set** and mark papers on paper" — the word that matters is
> **set**. Papers already exist. The job is not writing 20 questions, it is
> transcribing 20 questions that are already on a sheet. And transcription cost
> is entirely a property of your input UI.

**Premise it challenged:** P2, on three grounds — the 90-minute cost is a UI
artifact; reuse pays too far out to motivate adoption; and P2 and P3 are in
tension, since if reuse had sold the instructor, P3 would not be the decisive
risk.

**Accepted:** the cost argument and the P2/P3 tension, in full. P2 is revised
above.

**Rejected:** the payback argument. It rested on an assumed calendar the cold
read could not check and named as its own decisive test — "check `intakes` for
how often a given `course_id` actually recurs." Intakes are rolling, so reuse
lands inside the term.

**Also rejected:** its sequencing of the reuse counter last, on the grounds that
it is worthless until intake two exists. It is worthless to the *instructor*
until intake two; it costs two hours now, and deferring it to a moment months
away is how it never gets done. It is step 3 below, not last. (The cold read was
right that the change is bigger than "one SQL line" — see step 3 — but that
argues for doing it while the code is fresh, not for postponing it.)

## Approaches Considered

**Approach A — Pilot on what exists** (S, ~1 day human / ~2h CC). Commit,
migrate, soften the gate, lab pilot, no new features. Rejected: the instructor's
one and only first impression is twenty POST round-trips, and a bounce there
teaches you the tool failed without telling you whether the idea would have
worked.

**Approach C — The office types it in** (XS, ~1 day human / ~2h CC). The desk
transcribes the paper; the instructor's first experience is an already-marked
gradebook. Rejected: it never exercises the 649-line authoring screen, so the
component most likely to fail goes untested, and it sets an expectation that
transcription is someone else's job.

**Approach B — Fix the input, then pilot** — chosen, below.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B.** The importer costs the same four hours whenever it is built, and
it is worth far more before the instructor's first impression than after it.
Ordered, and the order is the argument — steps 1–5 have no dependency on a

### Step 2's import grammar, pinned

The format must be specified here, not left to the implementer, because every
axis below is already supported by the schema and a silent wrong guess changes
the stored paper:

```
Q. What does OSPF use to elect a DR? [2]
*Highest router priority
Lowest router ID
*Highest router ID as tiebreak
MAC address
> Priority is checked first; router ID only breaks a tie.

Q. OSPF is a distance-vector protocol.
True
*False
```

- `Q. ` starts a question. Blank line separates questions.
- `[n]` at the end of the question line sets `points` (1–100, matching the
  form's existing validation). **Absent means 1.** Without this a two-mark
  question on a real paper cannot be transcribed, which defeats the wedge.
- `*` prefixes a correct option. A literal leading asterisk is escaped `\*`, and
  **the parser strips the backslash before storing** — the option text saved is
  `*foo`, not `\*foo`.
- `> ` sets `explanation`. Optional.
- **Type is inferred and the inference is shown, never silent.** `truefalse` iff
  the option set is exactly True/False; `multiple` iff two or more options are
  starred; `single` otherwise.

  The risk here is narrower than an earlier draft of this document claimed, and
  the correction matters because it changes what the importer must do.
  `mpc_save_options()` (`admin/quiz-edit.php:85`) inserts whatever texts it is
  passed and then, for `single` **and** `truefalse` alike, keeps only the first
  correct option (lines 115–130). It does **not** synthesise True/False texts —
  that happens in the action handler at `admin/quiz-edit.php:235–238`, *outside*
  the function step 2 reuses. Two consequences for the implementer:

  1. The silent-corruption risk is real only for **multiple → single**: two
     starred options inferred as `single` loses one, permanently and quietly.
     `truefalse` and `single` behave identically inside `mpc_save_options()`, so
     confusing those two changes only the stored `type`, not the options.
  2. **The importer must supply the literal texts "True" and "False" itself**
     when it infers `truefalse`. Reusing `mpc_save_options()` does not get that
     for free, and the handler's own comment explains why it matters — otherwise
     one of them ends up spelled "Ture" on question 9.

- **The preview must display, per question: inferred type, points, and the
  question text *after* the points marker has been stripped.** Showing the
  stripped text is not cosmetic. `[n]` collides with legitimate question text —
  "How many bits in an IPv4 address? [32]" parses as a 32-point question, and 32
  is inside the valid 1–100 range, so nothing rejects it. The preview showing
  "How many bits in an IPv4 address?  ·  single  ·  32 points" is the only thing
  that makes that visible before commit.
- **There is no way to force a type, by design.** A multi-select question with
  exactly one correct answer is not representable and will be stored as
  `single`. That is an accepted limitation of a format optimised for pasting,
  not a bug to work around; an instructor who needs it should use the existing
  per-question form.
- **Import appends; it never replaces.** Existing questions on the quiz are
  untouched.
- **One bad question aborts the whole import** inside the existing transaction,
  and the preview names the offending question. Reject at preview time: zero
  starred options (which `mpc_publish_blocker()` would reject later anyway, far
  more expensively), duplicate option text, and option text over
  `VARCHAR(500)`.
- Accept CRLF as well as LF. The paper is coming out of Word.

**Test it.** Split the parser out as a pure function — text in, an array of
`{text, type, points, explanation, options[{text, correct}]}` out, no database —
and put it under `tests/QuizTest.php`. It is the one piece of this plan with a
trivially testable signature, and in a repo whose stated standard is
mutation-checked tests, shipping the instructor's only input path untested is
not consistent with the rest of the module. Minimum cases: the `[32]` collision,
`\*` unescaping, two-star inference to `multiple`, exactly-True/False inference,
zero stars rejected at preview, duplicate option text, an option over
`VARCHAR(500)`, and CRLF input.

## Open Questions

1. **Which instructor?** Asked twice, not answered. The build should not wait on
   it, but step 6 cannot be scheduled without it, and the assignment below is
   addressed to a specific person who is currently unnamed.
2. **What is a "point"?** `TODOS.md` item 3 raised it; still unanswered. The
   module scores papers but nothing combines a score with attendance. Not
   blocking — but if MPC ever announces "points" to students, this needs an
   answer first.
3. **Which student asked for the portal, and what did they say?** Open since
   2026-08-19 and recorded then as unanswerable. Still the cheapest available
   piece of real demand evidence.
4. **What is the actual fee data-entry latency?** Step 5's log-only mode is
   designed to answer this: every would-be refusal is recorded while the sitting
   proceeds. Reinstating enforcement needs the number, and now there is a
   mechanism that produces it instead of a promise to measure later.
5. **Does the parse format survive contact?** Instructors will paste things the
   importer does not expect. The preview in step 2 is the mitigation; whether it
   is enough is unknown until someone pastes a real paper.

6. **`mpc_quiz_gradebook()` still does best-attempt scoring, and it should not.**
   Found by the round-2 review of this document, not by the session. The
   gradebook query selects each student's attempt by
   `a.score_percent = (SELECT MAX(b.score_percent) …)` at `lib/quiz.php:812–816`.
   That is exactly the scheme this project's learnings record as resolved
   against: `mpc-explanation-column-breaks-best-attempt-scoring` (confidence
   9/10, adopted 2026-08-29) says **score the first attempt only**, because
   explanations shown after submission plus multiple attempts plus best-attempt
   scoring means attempt 1 reveals the answers and attempt 2 scores 100% — so
   points measure persistence, not knowledge. `quiz_attempts` is append-only, so
   this cannot be cleaned up after the fact.

   It is **latent, not live**: harmless while `max_attempts` is 1, wrong the
   moment an instructor raises it, and nothing in the UI warns them. Step 5
   edits this very query and would have walked past it.

   Not folded into a step because it is a defect in shipped code rather than
   part of this plan, and because the fix is a decision — first attempt, or
   `MIN(attempt_no)`, or something else — that deserves being made deliberately
   rather than inside a step about fee columns. **Decide it before any
   instructor sets `max_attempts` above 1.**

## Success Criteria

- One instructor transcribes one existing paper in **under 25 minutes**. This is
  the number that decides whether the revised P2 is right; if it lands near 90,
  the importer did not solve the problem it was built for. Caveat worth stating
  out loud: the 90-minute baseline is itself an estimate this document elsewhere
  calls an unverified artifact, so 25 is a target, not a measured improvement.
  The honest comparison is transcription time against the ~40 minutes of marking
  it replaces.
- One intake of 15–25 students sits that paper in the lab, supervised, with
  **zero students refused at the door** — and the log-only gate records **how
  many would have been refused** had it been enforcing. Zero refusals is the
  launch requirement; the recorded count is the finding, and a criterion of
  "nobody was refused" would be trivially true and measure nothing without it.
- `admin/quiz-grades.php` shows a complete gradebook, including the empty column
  for who did not sit.
- **The instructor uses the same paper for a second intake without being asked.**
  This is the only criterion that tests the reuse claim, and it is the one that
  matters most.

## The Assignment

Sit with the instructor while they transcribe one paper they have already
written. Time it. Do not help, and do not explain the screen — watch where they
stop.

Then ask them one question, in these words:

> "Which of these would make you open this again tomorrow — that tonight's stack
> is already marked, or that this paper will still be here for the next intake?"

Note the phrasing. P2 says marking and reuse are not in competition, and they
aren't — both are real and both arrive. The question is not which one to build,
it is **which one to lead with when you show it to the second instructor**.
Asking them to choose between the two as features would contradict the premise;
asking which one brings them back tomorrow is asking about order, and only they
can answer it.

Bring back the timing and the answer. Neither blocks any step above.
